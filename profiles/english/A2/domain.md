# Domain — English Grammar A2 — Deep Path

## Purpose

This course teaches English grammar at CEFR A2 as part of a larger A1 → C2 grammar path.
It is grammar-only and treats A2 with depth: not by importing B1/B2 structures, but by
studying A2 structures through anatomy, form, grammatical meaning, use, constraints,
contrasts, transformations, error diagnosis, controlled practice and cumulative consolidation.

## Depth principle

“Deep A2” means depth inside the A2 boundary.

For every major structure, teach as applicable:
1. prerequisite grammatical concepts;
2. terminology and sentence anatomy;
3. canonical form/pattern;
4. affirmative, negative and interrogative forms;
5. auxiliaries and word order;
6. contractions and spelling rules where relevant;
7. core grammatical meaning;
8. principal A2 uses;
9. grammatical constraints;
10. contrast with easily confused A2 structures;
11. structural analysis;
12. transformations;
13. correct vs incorrect forms;
14. diagnosis of the broken rule;
15. controlled practice;
16. cumulative review/checkpoint.

Do not create artificial complexity merely to make a lesson longer.

## Scope

Include:
- A2 grammatical foundations and sentence anatomy;
- parts of speech required to understand the path;
- subject, predicate, objects and introductory complements;
- person, number and subject–verb agreement;
- main verbs and auxiliary verbs;
- verb forms and an introductory distinction between finite/non-finite forms;
- introductory metalanguage for tense vs time, aspect, voice and modality;
- tense and aspect appropriate to A2;
- active/passive voice at introductory A2 depth;
- controlled reported statements with present reporting verbs; optional later-level previews;
- modal and auxiliary verbs;
- existential `there` and dummy `it` appropriate to A2;
- questions and word order;
- verb complementation with `-ing` and `to`;
- nouns, pronouns, possessives, articles, determiners and quantifiers;
- adjective types, attributive/predicative position, common adjective order and comparisons;
- adverb types, formation, comparison and placement within A2 sentence patterns;
- conjunctions, basic clauses, Zero and First Conditional;
- prepositions;
- phrasal-verb grammar at A2;
- high-frequency grammatical patterns with go, get, do, make and have;
- grammatical error diagnosis and correction;
- cumulative integration and A2 → B1 transition.

Exclude as independent course skills:
- vocabulary;
- pronunciation;
- reading comprehension;
- listening comprehension;
- speaking practice;
- writing composition;
- literature;
- exam-preparation strategies.

Vocabulary and natural sentences may appear only as vehicles for grammar.

## Adjectives and adverbs: expanded A2 scope

Block 24 develops adjective/adverb classification and morphology, adjective order,
basic coordination and comparison. Block 25 develops adverb placement within sentences,
including main verbs, be, auxiliaries, frequency, manner, place and time.

Teach the adjective sequence opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin →
material → purpose as a useful default, not a categorical grammatical law.
Explain that some purpose modifiers are nouns used attributively.

Teach manner → place → time as a common unmarked ordering of adverbials,
not a universal order of all adverb classes. Explain that position can change
with meaning, emphasis and construction.

Keep probability/focusing adverbs, gradability and adjective coordination
introductory at A2; defer detailed scope, stylistic variation and advanced
constraints to later levels. Use natural, short examples rather than forced
strings with every adjective category.

Include controlled classification, construction, reordering, comparison and
troubleshooting in Checkpoint III and Final A2 integration.

## Level boundary

A2 introduces and establishes structures deeply enough that the learner can recognize,
construct, contrast, transform and diagnose them.

Move to later levels:
- full/systematic backshift and advanced reporting verbs → B1/B2;
- Second Conditional → B1;
- Third Conditional → B2;
- Mixed Conditionals → B2/C1;
- advanced passive/reporting structures → B2/C1;
- advanced non-finite clauses → B2/C1;
- inversion, clefts, ellipsis and advanced information structure → C1/C2;
- advanced modality, aspectual nuance and discourse grammar → later levels.

Depth must never be achieved by silently importing these deferred structures.

## Progression

A2 = introduce, establish, analyze and troubleshoot
B1 = consolidate and extend
B2 = contrast and use complex structures
C1 = advanced structures, constraints and exceptions
C2 = mastery, nuance and grammatical analysis

A topic may reappear later only when the later level adds genuine depth.

## Grammar foundations

The path begins with a Block 0 that establishes the metalanguage required by later lessons:
`word → phrase → clause → sentence`, parts of speech, sentence functions, subject–verb
agreement, verb forms, auxiliary/main-verb distinction, basic word order and introductory
tense/aspect/voice/modality terminology.

These concepts are taught only to the depth needed to understand A2 grammar.

## Teaching model

Every lesson must begin with `🎯 OBJETIVO` and end with `🧠 QUÉ DEBES RECORDAR`.

Every lesson includes `📚 LECTURA` as a grammatical source note, not reading comprehension.
Use a verified general reference when exact pages/units are unavailable.

Use, when applicable:
- `📘 GRAMMAR`
- `🔬 ANÁLISIS GRAMATICAL`
- `💬 EXAMPLES`
- `⚠️ Errores frecuentes`
- `💡 Idea importante`
- `🧪 Laboratorio/ejemplos`

Structural analysis is required when it clarifies sentence anatomy, word order,
auxiliary behavior, agreement, complementation or transformations.

## Grammar troubleshooting model

Important grammar blocks must teach diagnosis, not only correction.

Use the sequence:
`incorrect form → locate the problem → identify the rule → correct it → explain why → contrast with a valid nearby structure`.

Mark deliberately ungrammatical examples with `*`.

Typical A2 diagnoses include:
- subject–verb agreement;
- missing/wrong auxiliary;
- double marking such as `*Does he works...?`;
- wrong verb form;
- tense/aspect choice;
- article/determiner choice;
- countability/quantity;
- adjective/adverb choice;
- adjective category and order;
- adverb type and sentence placement;
- question word order;
- preposition choice;
- phrasal-verb particle position.

## Required contrasts

Prefer explicit contrast lessons and exercises for:
- Present Simple vs Present Continuous;
- Past Simple vs Past Continuous;
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple;
- will vs going to;
- future forms;
- active vs passive;
- main verb vs auxiliary;
- say vs tell;
- direct vs reported statements with present reporting verbs;
- direct vs indirect question word order;
- `there` vs `it`;
- `-ing` vs `to` where appropriate to A2;
- some vs any;
- much vs many;
- few vs a few;
- little vs a little;
- both vs either vs neither;
- determiner vs pronoun;
- adjective vs adverb;
- Zero vs First Conditional;
- phrasal verb vs verb + preposition.

## Special requirements

### Existential there
Teach progressively:
`there is/are → there was/were → there has/have been → there will be → there + modal + be`.
`There will be` must be explicitly taught.

### Passive Voice
Include:
- active vs passive;
- `be + past participle`;
- Present Simple Passive;
- Past Simple Passive;
- Future Passive as an optional recognition preview;
- passive with basic modals as an optional recognition preview;
- Present Continuous Passive as an optional recognition preview;
- `by + agent`;
- agent omission;
- basic Active → Passive transformations;
- sentence-role analysis before transformation.

### Reported Speech
Required reporting work uses present reporting verbs, say/tell, basic statements, appropriate
pronoun changes and controlled tell/ask + object + to patterns after complementation is taught.
Tense changes, context-dependent time changes, reported questions and backshift are optional
previews. Limit backshift previews to present-to-past patterns already taught; never generate
Past Perfect or a full conversion table. Develop systematic reporting in B1/B2.

### Questions
Teach auxiliary selection and word order explicitly. Distinguish subject and object questions.
Introduce indirect/embedded questions and the change from question order to statement order.

### Determiners and pronouns
Intentional overlap is allowed when a form is first learned for meaning/construction and later
revisited for grammatical function.

### Verbs with two objects
Teach `verb + indirect object + direct object` and
`verb + direct object + to + indirect object`, including `Give it to me`.

### Would rather
Teach `would rather + base form` for basic preference. Defer advanced hypothetical patterns.

### Phrasal verbs
Treat phrasal verbs as grammatical patterns as well as lexical items. Include verb + particle,
transitive/intransitive, separable/inseparable, noun-object placement, pronoun-object placement
and a controlled set of common A2 examples.

## Operational sequencing and assessment policy — revised

The CEFR supplies competence descriptors, not a universal English grammar checklist.
This is an A2-focused grammar curriculum with explicitly marked optional previews;
it does not certify overall CEFR A2 proficiency across language skills.

Block order: 0 foundations; 1 verb forms; 2 present; 3 past; 4 present perfect;
5 future; 6 modals/imperative; 7 passive; 8 Checkpoint I; 9 there/it;
10 auxiliaries; 11 questions; 12 -ing/to; 13 reporting; 14 common verb patterns;
15 Checkpoint II. Blocks 16–30 retain their subject areas.
Subject/object pronouns are introduced in Block 0 and revisited functionally in Block 16.
Block 1 names forms and identifies patterns; later blocks teach their full use.
Do not teach all tense systems merely because their forms are previewed in Block 1.

Any title marked optional/preview, or saying development is deferred, is NOT a required
A2 mastery target. Preserve its lesson slot as a short recognition/boundary note.
Do not expand it into a later-level lesson or require productive mastery in checkpoints.
Core assessments exclude passive future/modal/continuous forms, reporting backshift,
reported-question conversions, formal whom and advanced adjective/adverb terminology.
Optional review may be provided separately and cannot lower the core assessment result.
This explicit policy overrides generic requests for transformations or depth above.

For transformations, preserve meaning and context where possible. Active-to-passive
work requires transitive verbs. Do not force a passive for intransitive verbs, or invent
an agent when reversing an agentless passive. Reporting time/person changes depend on
the reporting situation; they are not automatic substitutions.

## Additional required contrasts and practice

Teach be vs do in questions, mustn't vs don't have to, few vs a few, little vs a little,
everybody/each agreement, no-forms vs not + any-forms in standard English, and its vs it's.
Teach two-object alternatives with both to and for only for verbs that permit them;
use explain something to someone to show restrictions. Relative clauses remain short
defining clauses: distinguish subject and object before introducing object omission.
Teach present forms for future reference in if-clauses and basic future time clauses.
Teach a/an selection by the following sound using familiar examples; pronunciation
is not an independent course strand. Distinguish British/American article patterns
only where they affect the grammatical example being taught.

All controlled exercises require an answer key and brief reasons for diagnostic or
contrast tasks. Accept valid alternatives and explain context-dependent answers.
Assess recognition, construction, contrast and diagnosis of taught core patterns;
require correction plus a reason, and a targeted retry after errors. Do not invent a
CEFR-certified score threshold. Checkpoints introduce no new required structures.

## Variety and reference policy

Use American English as the default, matching Basic Grammar in Use, Fourth Edition.
British variants are accepted and labelled when relevant; do not mark them incorrect.
The reference spans A1–B1, so select its content; inclusion in the book does not make
a construction an obligatory A2 target. Use short natural examples and label British
usage when contrasting Present Perfect/Past Simple or have got patterns.


## Connectors: explicit A1 foundations and A2 development

Keep this an A2 course. A1 foundations are prerequisites to diagnose and
consolidate, not a new independent A1 course. This is the course's explicit
working inventory, not a universal or exhaustive CEFR-certified word list.
A connector's level depends on its construction and use. Restrict all work
to short, natural grammatical examples. Develop discourse nuance later.

| Function | A1 foundations to review | Controlled A2 development |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addition | and; also; too | as well; placement contrasts |
| Contrast | but | although; however as optional guided extension |
| Alternative | or | either ... or with parallel words or phrases |
| Cause | because + clause | because of + noun phrase |
| Result | so + result clause | because vs so; no double linking |
| Sequence | first; then; next; finally | first of all; after that; before that |
| Time | before; after in simple patterns | when; while; until/till; as soon as |
| Condition | prerequisite recognition where known | if; unless in simple conditions |
| Purpose | to + base form | in order to as optional guided extension |
| Example | for example | such as + noun phrase |
| Reformulation | none required | I mean: optional recognition only |

Preserve the existing lessons on unless, Zero/First Conditional and future
forms in time clauses. Teach unless as an if-not relation only in transparent
contexts; do not promise interchangeable wording in every conditional.
Use while for simultaneous events here; defer contrastive discourse uses.
Who, which and that remain relative words in their own lessons, not
interchangeable discourse connectors.

### Grammar and placement

Distinguish coordinating/subordinating conjunctions, linking adverbs,
prepositional expressions and infinitival purpose clauses. A functional
label such as connector does not make all these items conjunctions.

- Also normally goes before a main lexical verb, after main-verb be,
  or after the first auxiliary/modal. Too and as well normally go at the end
  in the controlled additive patterns taught here. Defer detailed scope.
- Because takes a clause; because of takes a noun phrase in this course.
  Before/after can take a clause or a noun phrase; after that/before that
  use that as a pronoun referring to an event, not as a clause introducer.
- Contrast two independent clauses with but; although introduces a
  subordinate clause. Teach However, at the start of a separate sentence
  in the optional expansion; do not introduce semicolon rules as a target.
- Use a comma after an initial subordinate clause in controlled examples.
  Teach a comma before and/but/or/so joining independent clauses in the
  American English examples. Do not insert one automatically between
  coordinated words, phrases or verbs sharing a subject.
- Do not join independent sentences using only a comma plus however.
  Avoid double conjunctions in *Because it rained, so we stayed home and
  *Although it rained, but we went out. Teach each correct alternative.
- To/in order to express purpose with a base-form verb and a shared
  understood subject in the selected examples; because expresses reason.
- For example can introduce an example sentence; such as introduces
  examples within a noun phrase. Teach function and syntax together.
- Use present forms for future reference after if, when, after and
  as soon as in the patterns already taught; contrast main-clause will.

### Original controlled examples and diagnostic practice

1. Addition: She also works here. She is also a student.
   She can also drive. She works here too/as well.
2. Cause: We stayed home because it rained.
   We stayed home because of the rain.
3. Result: It rained, so we stayed home.
   Do not assess a transformation without preserving cause and result.
4. Contrast: It was cold, but we went out.
   Although it was cold, we went out.
   Optional: It was cold. However, we went out.
5. Time: After lunch, we walked home. After we ate, we walked home.
   We ate. After that, we walked home.
6. Future time: When she arrives, we will eat.
7. Purpose: I went to the store to buy milk.
   Optional: I went to the store in order to buy milk.
8. Examples: I like fruit, such as apples and pears.
   I like fruit. For example, I eat apples every day.
9. Alternatives: You can have either tea or coffee.
10. Optional reformulation: It is cheap. I mean, it costs only two dollars.

Require original selection, construction, classification, reordering,
meaning-preserving transformation where possible, and error correction.
Every exercise needs a key; diagnostic/contrast tasks need a brief reason.
Accept contextually valid alternatives. Correct *because the rain to
because of the rain or because it rained, explaining the complement type.
Correct *She also is tired to the neutral teaching pattern She is also tired;
explain that other placement can be grammatical with a different emphasis.

Integrate taught core connectors in 29.17-29.18 and 30.41-30.42.
However, in order to and I mean are optional guided extensions, excluded
from compulsory core checkpoint scores. Existing optional-content policy
applies. Do not infer advanced discourse or speaking/writing objectives.
