# Domain — English Grammar A2 — Deep Path

## Purpose

This course teaches English grammar at CEFR A2 as part of a larger A1 → C2 grammar path.
It is grammar-only and treats A2 with depth: not by importing B1/B2 structures, but by
studying A2 structures through anatomy, form, grammatical meaning, use, constraints,
contrasts, transformations, error diagnosis, controlled practice and cumulative consolidation.

## Depth principle

“Deep A2” means depth inside the A2 boundary.

For every major structure, teach as applicable:
1. prerequisite grammatical concepts;
2. terminology and sentence anatomy;
3. canonical form/pattern;
4. affirmative, negative and interrogative forms;
5. auxiliaries and word order;
6. contractions and spelling rules where relevant;
7. core grammatical meaning;
8. principal A2 uses;
9. grammatical constraints;
10. contrast with easily confused A2 structures;
11. structural analysis;
12. transformations;
13. correct vs incorrect forms;
14. diagnosis of the broken rule;
15. controlled practice;
16. cumulative review/checkpoint.

Do not create artificial complexity merely to make a lesson longer.

## Scope

Include:
- A2 grammatical foundations and sentence anatomy;
- parts of speech required to understand the path;
- subject, predicate, objects and introductory complements;
- person, number and subject–verb agreement;
- main verbs and auxiliary verbs;
- verb forms and an introductory distinction between finite/non-finite forms;
- introductory metalanguage for tense vs time, aspect, voice and modality;
- tense and aspect appropriate to A2;
- active/passive voice at introductory A2 depth;
- basic reported speech;
- modal and auxiliary verbs;
- existential `there` and dummy `it` appropriate to A2;
- questions and word order;
- verb complementation with `-ing` and `to`;
- nouns, pronouns, possessives, articles, determiners and quantifiers;
- adjective types, attributive/predicative position, common adjective order and comparisons;
- adverb types, formation, comparison and placement within A2 sentence patterns;
- conjunctions, basic clauses, Zero and First Conditional;
- prepositions;
- phrasal-verb grammar at A2;
- high-frequency grammatical patterns with go, get, do, make and have;
- grammatical error diagnosis and correction;
- cumulative integration and A2 → B1 transition.

Exclude as independent course skills:
- vocabulary;
- pronunciation;
- reading comprehension;
- listening comprehension;
- speaking practice;
- writing composition;
- literature;
- exam-preparation strategies.

Vocabulary and natural sentences may appear only as vehicles for grammar.

## Adjectives and adverbs: expanded A2 scope

Block 24 develops adjective/adverb classification and morphology, adjective order,
basic coordination and comparison. Block 25 develops adverb placement within sentences,
including main verbs, be, auxiliaries, frequency, manner, place and time.

Teach the adjective sequence opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin →
material → purpose as a useful default, not a categorical grammatical law.
Explain that some purpose modifiers are nouns used attributively.

Teach manner → place → time as a common unmarked ordering of adverbials,
not a universal order of all adverb classes. Explain that position can change
with meaning, emphasis and construction.

Keep probability/focusing adverbs, gradability and adjective coordination
introductory at A2; defer detailed scope, stylistic variation and advanced
constraints to later levels. Use natural, short examples rather than forced
strings with every adjective category.

Include controlled classification, construction, reordering, comparison and
troubleshooting in Checkpoint III and Final A2 integration.

## Level boundary

A2 introduces and establishes structures deeply enough that the learner can recognize,
construct, contrast, transform and diagnose them.

Move to later levels:
- full/systematic backshift and advanced reporting verbs → B1/B2;
- Second Conditional → B1;
- Third Conditional → B2;
- Mixed Conditionals → B2/C1;
- advanced passive/reporting structures → B2/C1;
- advanced non-finite clauses → B2/C1;
- inversion, clefts, ellipsis and advanced information structure → C1/C2;
- advanced modality, aspectual nuance and discourse grammar → later levels.

Depth must never be achieved by silently importing these deferred structures.

## Progression

A2 = introduce, establish, analyze and troubleshoot
B1 = consolidate and extend
B2 = contrast and use complex structures
C1 = advanced structures, constraints and exceptions
C2 = mastery, nuance and grammatical analysis

A topic may reappear later only when the later level adds genuine depth.

## Grammar foundations

The path begins with a Block 0 that establishes the metalanguage required by later lessons:
`word → phrase → clause → sentence`, parts of speech, sentence functions, subject–verb
agreement, verb forms, auxiliary/main-verb distinction, basic word order and introductory
tense/aspect/voice/modality terminology.

These concepts are taught only to the depth needed to understand A2 grammar.

## Teaching model

Every lesson must begin with `🎯 OBJETIVO` and end with `🧠 QUÉ DEBES RECORDAR`.

Use, when applicable:
- `📚 LECTURA`
- `📘 GRAMMAR`
- `🔬 ANÁLISIS GRAMATICAL`
- `💬 EXAMPLES`
- `⚠️ Errores frecuentes`
- `💡 Idea importante`
- `🧪 Laboratorio/ejemplos`

Structural analysis is required when it clarifies sentence anatomy, word order,
auxiliary behavior, agreement, complementation or transformations.

## Grammar troubleshooting model

Important grammar blocks must teach diagnosis, not only correction.

Use the sequence:
`incorrect form → locate the problem → identify the rule → correct it → explain why → contrast with a valid nearby structure`.

Mark deliberately ungrammatical examples with `*`.

Typical A2 diagnoses include:
- subject–verb agreement;
- missing/wrong auxiliary;
- double marking such as `*Does he works...?`;
- wrong verb form;
- tense/aspect choice;
- article/determiner choice;
- countability/quantity;
- adjective/adverb choice;
- adjective category and order;
- adverb type and sentence placement;
- question word order;
- preposition choice;
- phrasal-verb particle position.

## Required contrasts

Prefer explicit contrast lessons and exercises for:
- Present Simple vs Present Continuous;
- Past Simple vs Past Continuous;
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple;
- will vs going to;
- future forms;
- active vs passive;
- main verb vs auxiliary;
- say vs tell;
- direct vs reported speech;
- direct vs indirect question word order;
- `there` vs `it`;
- `-ing` vs `to` where appropriate to A2;
- some vs any;
- much vs many;
- few vs a few;
- little vs a little;
- both vs either vs neither;
- determiner vs pronoun;
- adjective vs adverb;
- Zero vs First Conditional;
- phrasal verb vs verb + preposition.

## Special requirements

### Existential there
Teach progressively:
`there is/are → there was/were → there has/have been → there will be → there + modal + be`.
`There will be` must be explicitly taught.

### Passive Voice
Include:
- active vs passive;
- `be + past participle`;
- Present Simple Passive;
- Past Simple Passive;
- Future Passive;
- passive with basic modals;
- Present Continuous Passive as an introduction;
- `by + agent`;
- agent omission;
- basic Active → Passive transformations;
- sentence-role analysis before transformation.

### Reported Speech
Keep A2 to direct vs reported speech, say/tell, basic statements, basic tense/pronoun/time
changes, reported questions, reported commands and basic backshift. Do not expand this into
the complete reporting system.

### Questions
Teach auxiliary selection and word order explicitly. Distinguish subject and object questions.
Introduce indirect/embedded questions and the change from question order to statement order.

### Determiners and pronouns
Intentional overlap is allowed when a form is first learned for meaning/construction and later
revisited for grammatical function.

### Verbs with two objects
Teach `verb + indirect object + direct object` and
`verb + direct object + to + indirect object`, including `Give it to me`.

### Would rather
Teach `would rather + base form` for basic preference. Defer advanced hypothetical patterns.

### Phrasal verbs
Treat phrasal verbs as grammatical patterns as well as lexical items. Include verb + particle,
transitive/intransitive, separable/inseparable, noun-object placement, pronoun-object placement
and a controlled set of common A2 examples.

## Checkpoints and integration

The curriculum must contain explicit checkpoints, not merely generate them implicitly:
- Checkpoint I — A2 Foundations;
- Checkpoint II — A2 Verb System and Clause Core;
- Checkpoint III — A2 Nominal System, Modification and Clauses;
- Final A2 Grammar Integration;
- A2 → B1 boundary review.

Checkpoints must combine identification, construction, contrast, transformation, error
diagnosis and controlled mixed practice.

## Language

Explanations may be in Spanish for clarity. English grammatical terminology and example
sentences remain in English. Terminology must be explained rather than assumed.
