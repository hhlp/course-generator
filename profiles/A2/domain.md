# Domain — English Grammar A2

## Purpose

This course teaches English grammar at CEFR A2 as part of a larger A1 → C2 grammar path.
It is grammar-first: form, grammatical meaning, contrasts, transformations, examples,
common errors and controlled practice.

## Scope

Include:
- grammatical forms and sentence patterns;
- tense and aspect appropriate to A2;
- active/passive voice at introductory A2 depth;
- basic reported speech;
- modal and auxiliary verbs;
- existential `there` and dummy/anticipatory uses of `it` appropriate to A2;
- questions and word order;
- verb complementation with `-ing` and `to`;
- nouns, pronouns, possessives, articles, determiners and quantifiers;
- adjectives and adverbs;
- conjunctions, basic clauses, Zero and First Conditional;
- prepositions;
- high-frequency grammatical patterns with go, get, do, make and have.

Exclude as independent course skills:
- reading comprehension;
- listening comprehension;
- speaking practice;
- writing composition;
- literature;
- exam-preparation strategies.

Examples are required even though the four language skills are not course domains.

## Level boundary

A2 introduces and establishes structures. It does not attempt to exhaust every structure.

Move to later levels:
- full/systematic backshift and advanced reporting verbs → B1/B2;
- Second Conditional → B1;
- Third Conditional → B2;
- Mixed Conditionals → B2/C1;
- advanced passive/reporting structures → B2/C1;
- advanced non-finite clauses, inversion, clefts, ellipsis and information structure → C1/C2.

## Progression

A2 = introduce and establish
B1 = consolidate and extend
B2 = contrast and use complex structures
C1 = advanced structures and exceptions
C2 = mastery, nuance and grammatical analysis

A topic already introduced at A2 may reappear later, but later lessons must add genuine
depth rather than merely repeat the A2 explanation.

## Teaching model

For each grammar point:
1. define the structure;
2. show its form;
3. explain its core grammatical meaning/use;
4. provide affirmative, negative and interrogative forms when applicable;
5. provide multiple natural examples;
6. contrast it with easily confused structures;
7. explain common errors;
8. provide controlled grammar practice;
9. finish with the required summary.

Prefer explicit contrasts such as:
- Present Simple vs Present Continuous;
- Present Perfect vs Past Simple;
- will vs going to;
- active vs passive;
- say vs tell;
- some vs any;
- much vs many;
- both vs either vs neither;
- adjective vs adverb;
- there vs it;
- determiner vs pronoun.

## Special requirements

### Existential there

Teach progressively:
`there is/are → there was/were → there has/have been → there will be → there + modal + be`.

`There will be` must be explicitly taught, not merely implied by the future block.

### Passive Voice

At A2 include:
- active vs passive;
- `be + past participle`;
- Present Simple Passive;
- Past Simple Passive;
- Future Passive;
- passive with basic modals;
- Present Continuous Passive as an introduction;
- `by + agent`;
- omission of the agent;
- basic Active → Passive transformation.

### Reported Speech

Keep A2 to:
- direct vs reported speech;
- say/tell;
- basic reported statements;
- basic tense/pronoun/time changes;
- reported questions;
- reported commands;
- basic backshift.

Do not expand A2 into the complete advanced reporting system.

### Determiners and pronouns

Intentional overlap is allowed. For example, `some/any/no`, `both/either/neither`,
and related forms may first be taught for meaning/construction and later revisited to
explain their grammatical function as determiners or pronouns.

## Language

Explanations may be in Spanish for clarity. English grammatical terminology and example
sentences should be retained in English. The learner should progressively become
comfortable with terms such as subject, object, auxiliary, determiner, pronoun,
past participle, clause and preposition.


## Additional A2 coverage

### Indirect / embedded questions
Introduce direct vs indirect question word order, including patterns such as
`Do you know where...?` and `I don't know what...`. Keep complex embedding for later levels.

### Verbs with two objects
Teach `verb + indirect object + direct object` and
`verb + direct object + to + indirect object`, including pronoun-sensitive forms such as
`Give it to me`.

### Would rather
Teach `would rather + base form` for basic preference. Defer advanced hypothetical uses.

### Phrasal verbs
Treat phrasal verbs as grammatical patterns as well as lexical items. Include verb + particle,
transitive/intransitive, separable/inseparable, noun-object placement, pronoun-object placement
and a controlled set of common A2 phrasal verbs. Do not turn the block into an unlimited
vocabulary catalogue.
